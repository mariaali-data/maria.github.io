Project 02 · Python · Power BI

Can a trend line predict share prices?

SC356 Capstone, University of the South Pacific, 2026Group project, 3 members

A linear regression model of the US (SPY) and New Zealand (FNZ.NZ) markets from January 2022 to September 2026, using New Zealand as a rough guide to how Fiji's small stock exchange might behave.

+70%
US market growth since Jan 2022. NZ barely moved.
0.91
R² of the US trend line, a strong fit on paper
7.1% / 4.0%
Trend-line error (RMSE), US vs NZ. A higher R² did not mean a smaller error.
r = 0.39
NZ follows the US the next day (same day r = 0.07)

Method

A Python pipeline in Jupyter downloaded daily prices, aligned time zones, removed duplicates, filled holiday gaps, flagged outliers and used Adjusted Close. An OLS trend was fitted to each market.

Finding

Both trends rise, but prices sit above or below the line for months (Durbin-Watson near 0), so the line looks more certain than it is. Tested on 2026 prices, the US trend only just beat a simple no-change forecast (7.7% vs 8.0% error); the NZ trend did worse.

So what for Fiji

Use a trend line to describe the past, not to predict prices. We recommended the SPX and RBF publish daily historical prices so the work can be repeated on Fijian stocks.

Can we trust the trend line? Five checks from our hypothesis test

CheckRuleUSNZ
FitR² ≥ 0.70Pass · 0.91Fail · 0.60
AccuracyRMSE ≤ 10% of average pricePass · 7.1%Pass · 4.0%
StabilityEvery year's RMSE ≤ 10%Fail · 14.4% in 2022Pass
No autocorrelationDurbin-Watson 1.5–2.5Fail · 0.02Fail · 0.03
Constant varianceBreusch-Pagan p ≥ 0.05FailFail

Each market met only 2 of 5 checks, so we did not reject the null hypothesis: a straight trend line is not a reliable predictor of either market.
